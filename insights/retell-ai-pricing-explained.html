Retell AI Pricing Explained: Voice Minutes, Add-ons and Total Cost

Retell AI publishes usage-based voice pricing with multiple billable components. Here is how to read the pricing page and estimate the cost of a configured phone agent.

By Solomon Jarman · FounderNovember 7, 20268 min read

Retell AI is a voice-agent platform used to build and operate conversational phone agents. Its pricing page is more detailed than a single flat per-minute figure: the total depends on the selected voice engine, language model, telephony arrangement and optional features. This guide explains the pricing structure so a business owner can ask better questions before signing up with a platform or an agency.

Understand the difference between a platform and a managed service

Retell provides underlying voice-agent infrastructure and configuration tools. A managed AI receptionist provider may use Retell as one component, then add business discovery, prompt and call-flow configuration, phone setup, testing, reporting and ongoing support. The provider's subscription is therefore not necessarily the same thing as Retell's underlying usage bill.

The components shown on the pricing page

Retell's published pricing page lists a pay-as-you-go voice-agent range and a detailed breakdown of possible components. These include voice infrastructure, text-to-speech, language-model usage and telephony. Depending on configuration, optional charges can also apply to items such as knowledge bases, denoising, safety guardrails, personal-information redaction and quality assurance.

Rates and model availability change. The current live pricing page should always be treated as the source of truth. Do not assume an old screenshot, reseller quote or third-party comparison still reflects the current rate.

Why there is no single universal cost per minute

Different agents can use different language models, voices and telephony routes. One configuration may prioritize a low operating cost; another may use a premium voice or a more capable model. The number displayed as “cost per minute” is meaningful only when the selected configuration and included components are understood.

When comparing quotes, ask the supplier to identify:

  • The model and voice selected for the agent.
  • Whether the quoted price includes telephony and number rental.
  • Whether add-ons are enabled and billed separately.
  • How call duration is measured and rounded.
  • Whether test calls, transfers and failed calls are included.
  • Who pays the platform directly and who invoices the customer.

A simple cost-estimation method

  1. Estimate monthly connected call minutes using actual call logs where possible.
  2. Choose the exact Retell configuration in its pricing calculator.
  3. Record the estimated per-minute components and any monthly add-ons.
  4. Add phone-number rental and any external carrier charges that apply.
  5. Add the agency or managed-service fee, if you use one.
  6. Repeat the estimate at a higher call volume to understand busy-month exposure.
Important distinction: Retell's platform price is not automatically the retail price a business pays an agency. An agency may include setup, support, monitoring and other services. Ask for an itemized explanation instead of comparing unlike products.

Frequently asked questions

Does Retell charge only for minutes?

Not necessarily. The pricing page lists multiple usage components and optional add-ons. Review the current calculator and account terms for the exact configuration you plan to use.

Can a business use Retell directly?

Yes, Retell offers a platform for customers building their own agents. Businesses that do not want to configure and maintain the system may prefer a provider that handles setup and ongoing service.

Does the platform price include my agency's support?

No. Platform charges and service-provider charges are separate commercial arrangements unless a provider explicitly bundles them. Get a written description of what support and maintenance are included.

Bottom line

Retell's detailed component pricing is useful because it makes clear that voice-agent economics depend on configuration. Compare like for like, use the official calculator and separate underlying platform costs from the cost of a managed service.
